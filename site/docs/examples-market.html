Examples

Market data

These examples process market data using rounding, deduplication, JSON field extraction and OHLC bars.

Price ticker#

Intermediate Two rules and an export. Prices arrive on MARKET.<SYM>; subscribers get a deduplicated mirror, and big jumps are fanned out to an alerts subject that is forwarded to a real NATS server.

delta returns the difference from the previous value on the same subject (0 on first sight), so the alert rule stays quiet until it has a prior price. (subject-token 1) pulls the symbol out of MARKET.AAPL to build a per-symbol alert subject. The export form forwards only alerts.market.>; the raw ticks and the stable mirror stay local.

OHLC bars#

Intermediate bar! folds samples into open / high / low / close bars per symbol, closing either every N ticks or on wall-clock boundaries.

The bar rule is :reentrant true only because the second rule consumes the derived MARKET.<SYM>.bar.close stream. Alerting on closed bars instead of raw ticks keeps the alert free of intra-bar noise. A subscriber to MARKET.AAPL.bar.> sees a burst of four messages per closed bar, in open / high / low / close order. In-progress bars are part of the snapshot, so a restart mid-bar resumes without losing the open, high and low collected so far.

A market data firehose#

Advanced This example splits JSON market data frames into scalar subjects, then applies price filters and alert rules. Subscribers select the fields they need by subject.

The json-massive example simulates a Massive-shape websocket feed (stocks, options, crypto, forex). It has two pieces: mock_producer.js, a synthetic producer that speaks the NATS protocol over a plain socket and publishes frames on subjects shaped <ev>.<symbol>, and massive.edn, the patchbay below. There is more background in the blog post.

For stock trades the whole conditioning layer is three rules, each feeding the next:

  1. (json-demux! ...) on T.* splits the frame into one subject per field: T.AAPL.p (price) and T.AAPL.s (size).
  2. A rule on the demuxed T.*.p rounds to 2dp, drops duplicates and mirrors to T.<SYM>.p.stable.
  3. A second rule on T.*.p alerts on a jump of 1.00 or more between consecutive ticks on the same symbol.

Rule 2 matches T.*.p, a subject that only exists because rule 1 emitted it. That works because rule 1 is marked :reentrant true. The same wildcard also matches options (T.O:AAPL250620C00200000), and crypto and FX repeat the pattern with their own thresholds.

The second AAPL trade rounds to the same 187.42, so the stable mirror stays quiet. The third moves 1.526 and raises alerts.trade.AAPL. The second BTC trade is a 1.08% move and raises alerts.crypto.BTC-USD.

A subscriber to T.*.p.stable receives rounded prices by symbol without parsing the original JSON frames.

Last prints for slow markets and the close#

A dashboard opening on Sunday afternoon wants to render the last print for every symbol it tracks. Nothing is publishing, the market is closed, and the patchbay has been idle for two days. Opt the stable mirror into the last-value cache:

Subscribe to $LVC.T.*.p.stable and the broker hands back the most recent rounded price for every symbol it has seen, then nothing else until trading resumes. The same subscribe works for a logger restarting mid-session, or a tool that wants the last print on a thinly-traded name where ticks arrive minutes apart. The cache stores the latest rounded price; squelch suppresses repeated publications of that value.

Already-aggregated channels#

The AM / XA / CA channels deliver server-side OHLC bars on delayed feeds, so the patchbay demuxes those frames into scalar o / h / l / c / v streams instead of recomputing bars from raw trades. If you only have a trade stream and need to build bars locally, use bar!.

Bridging to NATS#

Subscribers can connect directly to monoblok, or receive processed market data through an existing NATS cluster. Add an export form pointing at T.*.p.stable and JetStream on the far side stores the deduplicated, rounded prices, not the raw firehose:

A remote stream configured for these subjects stores rounded prices and alerts. Replayed messages have the same payload format as live output. Raw frames remain available to local subscribers.

Budgeting bar state#

Advanced bar! is cheap per subject, but it is stateful. A local bar builder keeps one in-progress bar slot for every distinct market/symbol subject the rule has seen. Thousands of symbols across tens of markets can therefore turn into tens or hundreds of thousands of long-lived slots.

The important dimension is MARKETS * SYMBOLS: increasing TICKS mostly reuses existing slots, while increasing the subject universe creates more retained patchbay state. The same reasoning applies to every stateful form: narrow the filter when you can, and measure the shape you plan to run.
